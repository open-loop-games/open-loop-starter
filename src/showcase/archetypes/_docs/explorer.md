## Player experience

Explorer is the archetype where _the map is the content_. You start in one sector with a fog of unknown around you, spend a stamina-like action resource to scout the next tile, and watch the world literally enlarge as you move. The first fifteen minutes are onboarding-via-cartography: you are learning what the symbols mean by uncovering them, and every step is a small reveal. Scarce resources (food, water, scrap metal) force you back to a base tile, then out again, and the session texture becomes a heartbeat of expedition → retrieve → refit → expedition.

A thirty-minute session is a widening circle punctuated by equipment moments. You find an item — a knife with +2 scavenge, a canteen that doubles water capacity, a torch that turns a dark sector survivable — and that item reshapes where on the map you can reach. The long-term pull is completion of a _place_: every sector eventually visited, every landmark investigated, every passage between levels charted. Unlike Looper, your progress does not reset at the end of a run; unlike Scaler, it does not compress. The map you charted an hour ago is still charted. The reward is territory, not a number.
