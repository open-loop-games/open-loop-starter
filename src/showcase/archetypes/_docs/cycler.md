## Player experience

Cycler is the archetype of _breathing in and breathing out_. The game divides into two named phases — a calm build phase where you place infrastructure on a shared map and spend what you've earned, and a pressure phase where whatever you built gets tested against an escalating threat. The phases share everything: the same coin pile, the same walls, the same fortress topology. Phase A sets the board; Phase B reveals whether the board holds. The rhythm is cyclical — day becomes night becomes day, round becomes wave becomes round — and each cycle raises the stakes, so Phase A must outpace Phase B's escalation just enough to stay ahead.

A thirty-minute session spans three to six full cycles. The signature moments are the transitions — the sunset, the "wave incoming" countdown, the horn before the assault — and the inspection beats that follow a survived wave ("the east wall held; reinforce the north"). Unlike Producer you are not chasing a central number; unlike Defender you are not committing once to an architecture and watching it auto-resolve; unlike Survivor you are not inside the threat. You are the architect during Phase A and the auditor during Phase B, and the long-term pull is the compounding payoff of shared-state investment: the fortress that survived ten nights is _yours_ in a way that no re-rolled run can match. Sessions end when the cycle finally overruns you, or — in campaign-closure variants — when the final wave is cleared and the run-arc closes.
