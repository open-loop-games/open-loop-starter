## Player experience

Looper is the archetype where _a run feels like a rehearsal_. You queue up a sequence of actions inside a strictly limited time budget (a mana pool, a health bar, a countdown), watch the budget drain as each action ticks, and when it's gone the run ends — most of what you built resets, and a thin layer of permanent mastery carries to the next run. The compulsion loop is: _"next run I'll do it faster and further,"_ and over six or eight runs you start doing things you couldn't even reach on run one.

A thirty-minute session is a sequence of queue edits. You notice a skill that's now underpowered, or a new zone you can just barely reach, and reorder your plan accordingly. Between runs you are never idle — you're looking at the queue, at the skill readouts, at the retention track, deciding what run N+1 buys you that run N didn't. The long-term pull is _compounded efficiency_: a first-run player takes ten minutes to reach the second zone; an experienced player reaches it in thirty seconds and spends the remaining mana somewhere deeper. The game is the diff between those two runs.
