## Player experience

Defender is the archetype of _committing architecture before threat arrives_. You lay towers, traps, or turrets on a grid; you spend a small currency pool on opening placements; then the first wave of enemies arrives and walks a path your layout helped define. You watch the outcome resolve without mid-wave commands. Between waves, you buy another tower, upgrade a range, reshape a lane, and commit again. The texture of play is _tension held across a pause_ — the wave is coming, you have thirty seconds to prepare, and once it starts the board tells you whether your architectural decision was right.

A thirty-minute play session is a sequence of wave-clear moments separated by build-phase decisions — the first tower planted, the first round where the last enemy dies before reaching the exit, the first upgrade that changes which enemy type you can handle, the first stage cleared. The long-term pull is vertical: later towers punch harder, later waves escalate to force new answers, and the roster of towers you know how to combine becomes your expressive vocabulary. Between stages, what you earned survives — a level that buys permanent skills, so a stage that beat you yesterday is winnable today. The player role is _architect_, not participant.
