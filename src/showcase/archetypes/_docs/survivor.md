## Player experience

Survivor is the archetype of _standing inside the threat_. You control a single character on a scrolling arena; enemies pour in from the edges and close toward you; your weapons fire automatically on cooldown and your only direct inputs are movement and per-level-up item choices. The texture of play is continuous reactive positioning — kite the horde, catch the XP gems, turn to feed the oncoming wave into your weapon's firing arc. Every thirty to ninety seconds, a level-up pauses combat and offers three or four item cards; you pick one, then the arena resumes with the threat a little denser and your build a little sharper. The player role is _participant_, not architect.

A thirty-minute play session is roughly one complete run. Enemy composition escalates on a minute-mark schedule; by mid-run you are chasing specific weapon-passive pairings that trigger named _evolutions_ — qualitative end-states that turn a level-8 base weapon into a distinct, stronger form. The run ends on a timer or on death, and run-earned currency flows into a between-runs shop of permanent bonuses. The long-term pull is the unlock cascade — new characters, weapons, stages, and modifier cards that reshape what future runs can build toward — plus a mechanical compound: each Power-Up makes the next run a little easier, so yesterday's 20-minute death becomes today's 30-minute completion.

The defining component — weapons that fire on their own while the player only chooses where to stand — has no research pattern. It stands as a candidate in `open-loop-research/patterns/candidate-patterns.md`, with Brotato's fire site among its evidence.
